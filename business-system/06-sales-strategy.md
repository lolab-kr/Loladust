# 6 — Sales Strategy

## 6.1 The funnel (Awareness → Interest → Conversion → Retention)

| Stage | Goal | Channels | The move | Success signal |
|-------|------|----------|----------|----------------|
| **Awareness** | Get discovered | Reels/TikTok, Google search, hashtags | 2 Reels/week + tiramisu ads (cheap reach) | Reach ↑, profile visits ↑ |
| **Interest** | Earn a follow / click | IG profile, website, drop teasers | Bio CTA, "This Week's Drop" page, flavour story | Follows, site visits, cart-adds |
| **Conversion** | First purchase | Shopify, WhatsApp, drop window | Scarcity (drop), bundles, easy checkout, PayNow | Orders, AOV, conversion rate |
| **Retention** | Repeat + referral | CRM, WhatsApp, email | Post-delivery follow-up, birthday reminders, VIP early access | Repeat rate, CLV, referrals |

**The on-ramp trick:** tiramisu is cheap to acquire and delicious → first purchase → CRM captures
them → they buy the $157 cake for the next birthday. *Low-AOV product buys you a high-AOV customer.*

## 6.2 Launch strategy (first 30 days)

1. **Pre-launch (week 1–2):** build the "coming soon" + waitlist. Tease on Stories daily. Collect
   emails/WhatsApp (seed the CRM). Goal: **100+ waitlist** before you sell anything.
2. **Founder's Drop (launch day):** limited first batch to the waitlist *only*, 24h early. Makes
   them feel chosen, guarantees a sell-out, creates your first "SOLD OUT" proof.
3. **Public drops (weekly from week 3):** open to all, the machine in `03-instagram-strategy.md`.

## 6.3 The limited-drop model (recap + the numbers)

- **Weekly drop:** 10–12 cakes + ~20 tiramisu, Sat window, public sell-out.
- **Monthly special:** themed, higher-priced, pre-order + waitlist (your margin + hype peak).
- **Never restock a sold-out drop.** Scarcity is the product.
- Every drop **feeds the waitlist** → the waitlist is your most valuable asset (owned audience,
  zero CAC on the next launch).

## 6.4 Upselling & bundling (the AOV levers)

Getting AOV from **$69 → $95** cuts the orders you need for $5k from 74 to ~53. This is the single
highest-leverage move in the whole system.

| Tactic | Offer | AOV effect |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| **Bundle** | "The Full Table": 6" cake + 4 tiramisu cups + candles + card — $189 (vs $210 apart) | +$32/order |
| **Add-on at checkout** | Candles ($6), card ($4), tiramisu cups (+$18), extra loaf (+$15) | +$10–20 |
| **Free-delivery threshold** | Free over $150 → pushes $130 carts to add one item | +$15–20 |
| **Tiered sizing** | Offer 8" (+$60) beside 6" — anchors 6" as "sensible" | shifts mix up |
| **Corporate/recurring** | Monthly office banana-loaf box; event bundles | high-AOV, low-CAC |

## 6.5 Pricing psychology & perceived value

- **Charm vs prestige:** for premium, use **round/whole prices** ($157, not $156.90) — round =
  confident/luxury; charm ($X.99) = discount. Keep the $157 (specific-but-whole reads *crafted*).
- **Anchor high:** show the 8" ($217) *first*, so the 6" ($157) feels reasonable. Show the
  sculpted-tier market price ("others from $200+") to frame yours as value-premium.
- **Bundle discount, never product discount.** Discounting the cake cheapens the brand; a bundle
  that "saves $21" raises AOV *and* perceived generosity without touching the anchor price.
- **Scarcity = value.** "12 made this week" justifies the price more than any adjective.
- **Justify the premium visibly:** "baked the morning of delivery," "small-batch," "hand-textured,"
  named local ingredients (Gula Melaka, Valrhona-grade chocolate). Perceived value is *specifics*.
- **Never discount to drive volume.** If a drop doesn't sell out, *shrink the batch*, don't cut the
  price. Protect the anchor at all costs — it's the whole positioning.

**Set your real floor price:** ingredient + packaging + delivery cost per cake × **~3–4** = retail.
If a 6" costs you ~$40 all-in, $157 is a healthy ~4× — good. Recompute per SKU before launch.
